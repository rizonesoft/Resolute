## The three questions

**Does it make sense?** Yes. This domain runs early on purpose: a bar written after the work is a description of the work. The profile is what every later section is built against.

**Is it logical?** After round three. Round two fixed the bar and the checker; round three found that **two of those fixes had not reached the summaries that describe them**, and that the checker still had a count where it needed a set. One clause forbade the entire purpose of a repair tool, and the checker that was supposed to catch that class of thing could be bypassed with two spaces.

**Did it break anything else?** No. 13 gates green where 11 were, 36 tests unchanged, tidy unchanged at 169 over 21 translation units. `D01 T01 §9` gained a reciprocal note and no other section changed behaviour.

## Findings

### F1 -- the measured defect classes are eight, not seven -- record -- CORRECTED in `a73addf` (self)

The section asked that "each of the seven measured defect classes maps to a clause". **Seven appears nowhere in the source it points at.**

The brainstorm record lists ten bullets under "Defects found while measuring". One is explicitly not a defect, the `.sni` build number being one behind the `.au3`, which is the auto-increment convention. Two are not per-tool conformance defects: the broken clean checkout is a build-system problem owned by `D00 T01 §4`, and the dead `Rescue/` directory is housekeeping. The remaining **eight** are what the profile is derived from.

Every figure was re-measured against `resolute_au3/` rather than quoted, and one was wrong. The Current state block said **five** tools had no language pack, while the brainstorm names four and then separately lists three more with English only. `Language/` holds ten tool directories plus `BuildSystem/`, so four of fourteen have none: `Chromin`, `Distro`, `Edgemin`, `Watermin`. Corrected in the block.

Two other measurements were confirmed exactly rather than assumed. Seven tools carry a `.lng` settings literal and no `.ini`, and six carry no `Includes\Logging.au3`. The first logging probe was wrong, counting `_Logging_Initialize` and finding ten, because `BiosCodes` and `MemBoost` carry private copies. **The wrong probe was itself evidence for the clause**, which is why C04 is stated as an absence.

### F2 -- the section was told to wait for a decision that has no owner here -- record -- ROUTED to `D01 T01 §9` in `a73addf` (self)

The brainstorm record says the standalone on-disk layout "needs reconciling **before** the conformance profile is written", because the profile has to assert where a standalone tool finds its files. That layout is owned by `D01 T01 §9`, which has not shipped.

Stalling was not an option and guessing would have put a second, drifting copy of the layout in this document. So **standalone-ness is stated as behaviour and names no paths**: C14 says a tool alone in an empty directory starts, localizes, shows About and checks for updates; C15 confines its own persistence; C16 says it needs no sibling tool.

That is true under the shipped suite's shared-root layout and under Complete Windows Repair's one-folder `Doors/` convention both, so whichever `D01 T01 §9` chooses, the profile does not need rewriting. The cost is written down in both places: the profile cannot check that a tool put its files in the *agreed* place, only that it needs nothing outside its own folder.

### F3 -- the checkpoint could not fail -- record -- CORRECTED before implementing (self)

It asked that the profile "states every clause in evaluable terms" and that a mapping "is quoted". Both are satisfied by a careless author asserting them, which is how a document gets signed off without being checked.

Every clause of the checkpoint is now something `scripts/profile-check.py` decides, and each refusal was **driven** rather than described. "Evaluable" is mechanical: a `Method` column from a closed set of seven, so a clause whose method is `reasonably` fails by name.

### F4 -- C15 forbade the entire purpose of a repair tool -- correctness -- FIXED in `e02cb85` (independent)

Raised as a **P2** by the independent review, with the source line, and it is the worst finding here because **the bar itself was wrong**.

C15 read "the tool writes nothing outside its own folder." `ComIntRep` restores the hosts file:

```
ComIntRep.au3:2465   Local $sPathHOSTS = @WindowsDir & "\System32\drivers\etc\hosts"
                     FileCopy($sPathHOSTS, $sPathHOSTS_Backup)
                     _FileEx_FileDelete($sPathHOSTS)
```

Verified at the cited lines. That effect is **frozen** under `AGENTS.md`, so the clause forced a choice between a tool that fails the profile and a tool that changed a frozen behaviour.

**The mistake was generalising the right intent to the wrong scope.** The measured AutoIt defects were about *persistence in the wrong place*: a settings file at a shared root, a log the standalone tool cannot find. I wrote that as "writes nothing outside its folder" and swept up the product itself.

C15 now confines the tool's own persistence and is traced on a session performing no repair. Repair writes are not exempted but **governed more strictly**: new C33 requires every outside path to be a declared repair target and nothing else, on top of C27, C28, C29 and C30. A repair tool now owes more about its outside writes than a read-only tool owes about having none. The reasoning sits in the profile, so the clause cannot be re-broken by somebody reading the row alone.

### F5 -- two spaces bypassed the checker entirely -- adversarial -- FIXED in `e02cb85` (independent)

Raised as a **P2**, reproduced by the reviewer.

`CLAUSE_ROW` anchored at the line start, so an **indented** table row was skipped. Markdown renders it identically, so a clause stayed visible to every human reader and invisible to the check. Indenting C31 and clearing its owner produced `31 clause(s) ok`.

**This is the defect the script exists to prevent, one level up.** `D00 T01 §1`, `§2`, `§4` and `§5` each found a script reporting success while doing nothing; `D00 T02 §4` found a parity instrument that could not tell failing to observe from observing no difference. Here I built the instrument that has it. Writing a checker does not exempt the checker.

Fixed, and driven with the reviewer's own reproduction:

```
line 73: clause C31 names no owner section
```

### F6 -- the checker had nothing exercising it -- test-coverage -- FIXED in `e02cb85` (independent)

The reviewer asked for a regression test, and F5 is the argument for it: I had driven five failure modes by hand, and none of them was an indented row, because I do not think of rows as indentable.

The repository already had the convention. `--self-test`, beside `todo-graph.py self-test` and `todo-claims.py --self-test`, wired as its own gate. **15 refusal cases and 1 positive control**, each refusal a minimal profile correct except for one thing.

**Each case asserts the problem is NAMED**, not merely that the exit is non-zero. A case checking only the exit code would pass on a checker that rejected everything, which is the same unfalsifiability F3 was about.

### F7 -- the C15 fix did not reach the two places that summarise it -- consistency -- FIXED in round 3 (independent)

Raised as a **P2** by the round-3 review of the stamp commit, and it is the sharpest of the four.

F4 corrected C15 itself. It did not correct `docs/conformance-profile.md:116` or `todo/07-quality/TODO-01-quality-bar.md:88`, both of which still said the tool "writes nothing outside its own folder". So **the defect remained reproducible from the checked-in record** while a findings file declared it fixed: a repair-tool implementer reading the section's own Done note, or the profile's own summary of its scope, would have been taught the rule the clause no longer states.

The lesson is narrow and worth keeping: **a fix lands on the clause, and the clause has restatements.** Correcting a rule means correcting everything that paraphrases it, and the paraphrase is exactly what a reader reaches first.

Both corrected, each keeping the old wording inside a dated `Corrected:` note, because the next reader needs to know it moved.

### F8 -- the checker counted defect classes instead of identifying them -- correctness -- FIXED in round 3 (independent)

Raised as a **P2**. `evaluate()` tested `len(defects) == 8`, so renumbering `D8` to `D9` left eight unique rows, an empty problem list, and a gate reporting **all classes closed** while a measured class had silently left the profile.

This is the same defect as F5 one level further up: F5 was a check that did not look at a row, F8 is a check that looked at the **shape** of the evidence rather than the evidence. Both report success on something they never examined.

Now `EXPECTED_DEFECT_IDS` is the exact set, and both directions are named: a class that left the profile, and a class that was never measured. Two self-test cases added, taking the suite to 16.

### F9 -- the stamp's own inventory was wrong -- record -- FIXED in round 3 (independent)

Raised as a **P2**. The stamp said "23 distinct sections in **five** domains"; the owners span `D00`, `D01`, `D02`, `D06`, `D07`, `D08`, which is **six**. The section's Done notes still said **32 clauses** and **8 repair** after the review added C33.

**The stamp was written before the round-2 fix and not recomputed after it.** Flipping the row to `[x]` published those figures as canonical, which is worse than leaving them in a draft: a stamp is what a later reader trusts instead of re-deriving.

### F10 -- the self-test was described as 14 refusal cases and is not -- record -- FIXED in round 3 (independent)

Raised as a **P3**, and correct. The suite contains one **positive control**, `a correct profile passes`, and the rest are refusals. Describing all of them as "each a minimal profile correct except for one thing" overstated the refusal coverage by one.

Small, and worth fixing precisely because this file is cited as refusal-coverage evidence. The count is now **15 refusal cases and 1 positive control**, and the control is worth naming rather than hiding: without it, a checker that rejected every input would pass the whole suite.

## What the profile actually is

```
profile-check: 33 clause(s) ok -- 24 universal, 9 repair
  -- 8 measured defect class(es), all closed
  -- 23 owner section(s), all resolve
  D1 closed by C02, C03      D5 closed by C08
  D2 closed by C04, C05      D6 closed by C12
  D3 closed by C09           D7 closed by C21
  D4 closed by C07, C08      D8 closed by C22
```

Three properties are worth naming because a weaker document could claim to be the same thing.

**Every clause has an address that resolves.** Not a well-formed reference: the check runs each owner through `todo-graph.py resolve`, the same front door the skills use. `D08 T01 §77` is refused. That is `D00 T02 §4`'s lesson applied deliberately, that a reference in valid form pointing at nothing reads as an address and is not one.

**Every clause closes a measured defect or is stated as a requirement with a method.** A defect no clause closes fails the check, so the profile cannot quietly stop covering what it was derived from.

**The design values are not here.** C17 adopts `DESIGN.md` by reference and the check scans the whole document, prose included, for a hex colour or a pixel size. The cheaper substitute is not avoided by discipline, it is refused by the gate.

## Independent review

**Round 2.** `codex review --commit a73addf` (gpt-6-astra, high effort) returned **two P2, both right**, one found by reading `ComIntRep.au3` and one by mutating the checker.

**Round 3.** The reviewer changed to `gpt-5.6-sol` at high effort, pinned in the command rather than taken from the machine's config, and its first run was against **the stamp commit** `235a71f`. It returned **three P2 and one P3, all four right**.

That run is worth recording for what it chose to read. It reviewed the **record** rather than the code: two findings are a fix that did not reach its own restatements, one is a stamp publishing figures the previous round had made stale, and one is a findings file overstating its own evidence by a single case. The runtime gates were green throughout, and the reviewer said so before saying the stamp did not meet the repository's own contract.

**A stamp is a commit, and reviewing it is not optional politeness.** The previous convention held that a stamp commit "carries only this stamp" and was left unreviewed by design, recorded in `D00 T03 §1`. Round 3 is the counter-example: the stamp was the artifact with four defects in it.

Eleven sections, eleven times. What is different here is that **the subject was a quality instrument**, and it had both failure modes the instrument was written to prevent: a rule that is wrong, and a check that passes without looking. Writing the bar does not put you above it.

The standing lesson, extended once more. `§6`: my probes test the states I designed for. `D00 T02 §1`: a defect the reviewer already found once is the cheapest probe available. `D00 T02 §3`: a hazard I have written a paragraph about is not a hazard I have fixed. `D00 T02 §4`: I checked that the instrument reports correctly and never that it could see. **This one: I drove five failure modes by hand and none was an indented row, because I do not think of rows as indentable. Hand-driven probes are bounded by the author's imagination, which is the argument for the self-test rather than a nice-to-have beside it.**
